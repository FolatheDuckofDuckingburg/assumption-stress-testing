# Theoretical Particle Physics

## Purpose

This research area studies the fundamental particles, interactions, symmetries, and mathematical models used to describe particle phenomena. It focuses on the theoretical structure and physical implications of particle models, rather than treating particle physics as interchangeable with quantum field theory.

Theoretical particle physics draws heavily on quantum field theory (QFT), group theory, representation theory, and mathematical physics. QFT is a central framework for constructing and analysing many particle theories, while theoretical particle physics also includes model building, symmetry principles, phenomenology, and the interpretation of experimental constraints.

## Research directions

- **Standard Model:** particle content, gauge structure, interactions, parameters, and consistency.
- **Gauge symmetries:** local and global symmetries, gauge groups, representations, and their physical consequences.
- **Electroweak theory:** electroweak unification, symmetry breaking, and the Higgs mechanism.
- **Strong interactions:** quantum chromodynamics, colour symmetry, confinement, and hadronic structure.
- **Neutrino physics:** mass generation, flavour mixing, oscillations, and possible extensions.
- **Beyond the Standard Model:** carefully specified extensions, their internal consistency, and their testable consequences.
- **Particle phenomenology:** connecting theoretical models to measurable quantities and experimental constraints.
- **Particle cosmology:** links between particle models and the early universe, where relevant.

These are prospective directions, not claims of completed research or expertise.

## Assumptions to examine

Depending on the specific question, an investigation may examine:

- The choice of gauge group and particle representations.
- The role and implementation of spacetime and internal symmetries.
- Assumptions about field content, particle masses, and coupling parameters.
- Symmetry breaking mechanisms and their mathematical consistency.
- Perturbative assumptions and the limits of approximation methods.
- Model extensions, parameter spaces, and compatibility with existing constraints.
- Whether a proposed mathematical modification produces distinct, physically meaningful predictions.

An assumption should be isolated and defined precisely before it is varied. A different model or equation is not, by itself, evidence that the modification is physically viable or novel.

## Mathematical foundations

Relevant prerequisites and tools include:

1. Linear algebra and complex vector spaces.
2. Classical mechanics, special relativity, and electromagnetism.
3. Multivariable calculus and differential equations.
4. Group theory, Lie groups, and Lie algebras.
5. Representation theory and tensor methods.
6. Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics.
7. Quantum mechanics.
8. Quantum field theory, including canonical and path-integral formulations.
9. Gauge theory and the Standard Model.

The sequence is indicative; the required background depends on the investigation.

## Research standards

Each study should:

- State the physical question, model, conventions, and regime of validity.
- Define fields, symmetries, representations, parameters, and units.
- Derive claims from explicit assumptions and established mathematical frameworks.
- Check dimensions, symmetries, conservation laws, limiting cases, and consistency conditions.
- Compare with relevant literature and existing experimental constraints.
- Distinguish formal consistency, theoretical plausibility, phenomenological viability, and empirical confirmation.
- Record computational methods and parameters where calculations are used.

Mathematical consistency is necessary but does not establish that a model describes nature. Any claim of novelty requires a literature comparison and a clearly identified contribution.

## Relationship to other AST areas

- **Quantum field theory:** provides central mathematical tools for describing relativistic quantum fields and interactions.
- **Cosmology:** supplies contexts in which particle models may have implications for the early universe.
- **Quantum gravity:** raises questions about the relationship between quantum fields, spacetime, and gravitation.
- **Mathematical physics:** supplies structures and methods used throughout this research area.

These connections do not make the areas interchangeable; each should retain its own questions and standards.

## Initial study strategy

Begin with a structured reproduction of established results before proposing extensions. A suitable first sequence is:

1. Review the Standard Model's particle content and gauge structure.
2. Study the mathematical role of symmetry groups and representations.
3. Reproduce selected derivations from electroweak theory and quantum chromodynamics at an appropriate level.
4. Examine how a clearly specified model produces observable quantities.
5. Identify one narrowly defined assumption for a later stress-testing investigation.

No specific extension or novel result is presumed by this roadmap.

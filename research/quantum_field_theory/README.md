# Quantum Field Theory

## Purpose

Quantum field theory (QFT) is a mathematical framework for describing quantum fields and, in relativistic settings, their interactions. It is foundational to the Standard Model and many areas of theoretical particle physics, but it is not identical to particle physics as a research discipline.

This area focuses on the mathematical and conceptual structure of QFT: its formulation, symmetries, quantization, approximations, and domain of validity.

## Research directions

- **Classical field theory:** fields, actions, Euler–Lagrange equations, and Noether's theorem.
- **Canonical quantization:** operator fields, commutation relations, and Fock space.
- **Path-integral methods:** functional integrals, generating functionals, and perturbative expansions.
- **Relativistic and gauge field theories:** Lorentz symmetry, gauge invariance, and constrained systems.
- **Renormalization:** regularization, renormalization procedures, scale dependence, and renormalization-group ideas.
- **Effective field theory:** scale separation, effective operators, and controlled approximations.
- **Non-perturbative methods:** questions and techniques beyond straightforward perturbation theory.
- **Mathematical foundations:** distributions, functional analysis, geometry, and operator structures relevant to field theory.

These are prospective directions, not claims of completed research.

## Assumptions to examine

A study may investigate the role and limits of assumptions such as:

- Locality and Lorentz invariance.
- The choice of fields, degrees of freedom, and spacetime dimension.
- Symmetry principles and gauge redundancy.
- Perturbative expansions and their domains of validity.
- Regularization and renormalization prescriptions.
- Vacuum structure and boundary conditions.
- The use of effective descriptions at a given energy scale.

Any modification must be formulated precisely and checked for mathematical consistency before physical interpretation.

## Mathematical foundations

Relevant preparation includes:

1. Multivariable calculus and linear algebra.
2. Classical mechanics and variational calculus.
3. Special relativity and classical field theory.
4. Complex analysis and differential equations.
5. Group theory, Lie groups, and representation theory.
6. Quantum mechanics.
7. Classical field theory and Hamiltonian methods.
8. Distributions and functional methods.
9. Relativistic quantum field theory.

The appropriate sequence depends on the problem.

## Research standards

Each investigation should define its fields, action, symmetries, conventions, approximations, and regime of validity. Derivations should be checked for dimensional consistency, symmetry properties, conservation laws, and appropriate limiting behaviour. Computational results should include reproducible methods and parameters.

A formal construction is not automatically a physically viable theory. Claims must distinguish mathematical consistency, approximation quality, phenomenological relevance, and empirical support.

## Relationship to other AST areas

- **Theoretical particle physics:** uses QFT extensively to formulate particle models and calculate physical predictions.
- **Cosmology:** applies quantum-field-theoretic ideas in cosmological settings, subject to the assumptions and limitations of each treatment.
- **Quantum gravity:** investigates questions that arise when quantum theory and dynamical spacetime must be considered together.
- **Mathematical physics:** develops mathematical tools and structures relevant to field theory.

QFT is a framework shared across several research areas, not a substitute name for any one of them.

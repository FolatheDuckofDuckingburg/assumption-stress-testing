# Causal Set Theory

## Scope

Causal set theory is an approach to quantum gravity in which spacetime is modelled fundamentally by a **causal set**: a set of elements equipped with a partial order that represents causal precedence and satisfies local finiteness.

For a causal set \((C,\\prec)\):

- **Partial order:** the relation \(\\prec\) is transitive and irreflexive (with the associated reflexive order convention also used in the literature).
- **Acyclic causal structure:** the order encodes precedence rather than arbitrary pairwise connectivity.
- **Local finiteness:** for any related pair of elements, the interval between them contains finitely many elements.

The central idea is that causal order captures causal structure, while the number of elements in a region can encode spacetime volume. In suitable circumstances, these ingredients may support the recovery or approximation of continuum spacetime geometry.

This is a research framework, not an established demonstration that physical spacetime is discrete. AST will examine its mathematical foundations, assumptions, results, limitations, and empirical status without presuming that the framework is correct.

## Core mathematical ingredients

1. **Partially ordered sets:** definitions and properties of partial orders, intervals, chains, antichains, and order-preserving maps.
2. **Local finiteness:** the finiteness condition on order intervals and its consequences.
3. **Causal structure:** the relationship between causal order and the light-cone structure of a Lorentzian spacetime.
4. **Counting and volume:** how element counts can represent spacetime volume in a continuum approximation.
5. **Sprinkling:** probabilistic methods, particularly Poisson sprinkling, used to sample causal sets from continuum spacetimes while respecting Lorentz invariance statistically.
6. **Continuum approximation:** conditions under which a causal set can approximate a continuum spacetime, and the limits of such approximations.
7. **Dynamics:** proposed growth laws, actions, and other approaches to defining causal-set dynamics.

## Questions for investigation

- Which properties of a Lorentzian spacetime are determined by its causal order, and what additional information is needed?
- Under what conditions can a causal set admit a faithful embedding into a continuum spacetime?
- How are continuum volume and dimension estimated from order-theoretic data?
- Which features of a causal set are invariant under relabelling?
- How do discreteness and local finiteness constrain candidate dynamics?
- What are the mathematical and physical limitations of continuum approximations?
- Which predictions, if any, could distinguish causal-set models from continuum approaches?

These are open research questions, not claims that AST has solved them.

## Assumptions to stress-test

AST's approach is to make assumptions explicit and examine their consequences. Relevant assumptions include:

- that causal precedence is a fundamental organizing relation;
- that the underlying order is locally finite;
- that continuum Lorentzian geometry can emerge as an approximation;
- that element counts provide a meaningful correspondence with spacetime volume;
- that a suitable dynamics can be defined and connected to physical observables.

For each assumption, an investigation should state its precise mathematical form, identify the results that depend on it, and determine whether relaxing or replacing it preserves consistency and physical interpretability. A modified structure is not automatically a viable physical theory.

## Suggested study sequence

1. Review order theory and the mathematical definition of causal sets.
2. Study causal structure in Lorentzian geometry, including causal intervals and the role of dimension.
3. Understand faithful embeddings and the correspondence between order and volume.
4. Examine Poisson sprinkling and the role of Lorentz invariance.
5. Reproduce selected established results from the causal-set literature.
6. Investigate candidate dynamics only after the kinematical foundations are understood.
7. Assess physical interpretation, limitations, and possible observational consequences.

## Research standards

Work in this area should:

- define the order relation and conventions precisely;
- distinguish exact mathematical statements from probabilistic or asymptotic claims;
- specify the continuum spacetime and assumptions used in any embedding or approximation;
- document derivations, simulations, parameters, and references;
- compare proposed results with established causal-set literature;
- distinguish mathematical consistency, physical plausibility, and empirical evidence.
